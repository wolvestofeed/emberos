## The note

I have spent most of my working life somewhere between the soil and the table.

After college I lived on a biodynamic, organic, vegan CSA farm in the middle of nowhere New Mexico [✓ verify]. I learned that a farm is a living system before it is a business, and that the soil keeps score long after the season ends. Since then I have owned a pizzeria in Philadelphia and a 150-seat bar and grill in Boulder [✓ verify]. I have planned gleaning crews for heritage apple orchards in southwest Colorado, routing the fruit to food banks, community kitchens, cider and dried apple chips [✓ verify]. I have written SOPs and costed recipes for the region's kettle co-packer [✓ verify], grown microgreens on wire racks at Vallecito Micro Farm in Bayfield [✓ verify], and stood in the Colorado State Capitol for restaurant owners on labor, health care and the polystyrene ban [✓ verify].

Every one of those places taught me the same thing. Good food is a chain, and every link matters. Seed, soil, water and light. The hands that sow and harvest. The kitchen that turns a crate of produce into a meal someone looks forward to. The table where people gather and are fed, body and spirit. Break a link and the whole thing gets weaker: less nourishing, more wasteful, harder to make a living from.

I stand for physical, emotional and spiritual wellness, and for small businesses and healthy food. That has never changed. What I kept running into was how the people doing this work are asked to run it. The software is disconnected, so no one ever sees the whole business at once. Everything is managed to the bottom line, and the things that make a food business worth running get squeezed out: investing in people, building a culture the workforce wants to stay in, caring for the health of the people being fed. The health of the planet belongs on that same ledger, and until now no one has had a practical way to put it there.

So I built the system I always wanted.

Ember OS runs a food business from the seed to the plate in one place. It plans what to grow and what to cook from the orders that are actually coming in. It costs every tray and every batch from real prices paid. It keeps books a CPA would sign. It measures energy, water, waste and carbon in the same breath as production, because the planet's health and the business's health are the same account. And it shows its work. Every figure tells you where it came from, and a number that is still a guess says so, out loud, until the real one arrives.

Muse Kitchen is the first application, built for scratch commissary kitchens. Cotyledon is the second, built for growers of microgreens and sprouts, and it runs my own grow room. They speak different trades but share one engine and one set of values.

This is for the chefs, farmers, cooks, bakers, caterers and community kitchens who believe food can heal people and the places we call home, and who are tired of running that belief on inadequate software. Healthy soil grows nourishing food. Nourishing food builds healthy people.

Food is my muse. I hope Ember OS helps it become yours.

— Rob Bogatin
